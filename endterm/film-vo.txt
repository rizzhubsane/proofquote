Nine forty at night. Ravi does inside sales at a steel distributor in Ambala. Sharma Traders wants four hundred kilos of M S angle and twenty litres of primer. Then a WhatsApp: twelve percent lagao, eighteen nahi chalega.

Ravi's price list says G S T eighteen percent. The draft he is about to send says twelve. It reads like a normal quote. Ask a chatbot to tidy it up, and the twelve comes back cleaner. And what is on the quote becomes the invoice.

Proofquote takes two things. This week's price list, which is the only source of truth. And the draft.

Press check. For every line in the draft, Proofquote finds the price list line for that item, and checks that every rupee amount, every G S T rate and every H S N code is on it. Four steps, in order, once. The lookup is plain code, not a model, so no prompt can talk it into twelve percent.

Three lines pass. Two don't. Twelve percent is not on the list line for M S angle. The list says eighteen. The fifteen percent festive scheme isn't there either. The list says no scheme this week.

Proofquote never types eighteen in for you. Ravi removes the lines, or adds the missing line to the list if it is real. Until then, send stays shut.

Then n eight n emails the checked quote to the sales lead. Every rate sits next to the list line behind it, and what was removed is shown, struck through.

Other tools write the quote. Proofquote checks it. No list, no rate.
